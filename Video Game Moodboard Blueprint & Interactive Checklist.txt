Premise & Worldbuilding
Set in a hyper-accelerationist, techno-futurist society, the world is defined by non-stop momentum, greed, and commodification. Space itself is entirely leased and owned—nothing truly belongs to the individual. In this environment, the protagonist is driven by a desperate, manic urge to master his surroundings through art, construction, tools, and social hierarchy. Because he exists in a space where nothing abides or outlasts him, every effort feels hollow, causing his creative drive to repeatedly collapse into compulsive, solitary feedback loops and mechanical self-gratification.


The Chemical Spiral & Chemical Gentrification
To sustain his manic output, the protagonist relies on an advanced performance-enhancing drug that temporarily elevates his focus and shields his ego:


* The Synthetic Cycle: The substance fuels intense bursts of building and painting, but as the reality of his leased environment sets in, the crash triggers cognitive overload, forcing him into self-soothing behaviors to regulate his nervous system.
* * Chemical Gentrification: As the accelerationist system nears a breaking point, the ruling elite enclose the drug supply, turning it into an exclusive commodity reserved only for the top tier of society.
* * The Dream Counterpart: In his drug-altered state, dreams of an ancient autumn valley filled with 50-foot blood-marked statues haunt him. Consciously, his drug-fueled ego rejects the statues' message of sacrifice, dismissing himself as merely a "selfish animal" while placing false hope in techno-futurist promises.
* The Betrayal & Loss of Sanctuary
* The Walk: Seeking an escape, the protagonist leaves his room to walk through a simulated city park, seeking a fleeting sense of nature. He notices police nearby but ignores them, numb to the encroaching state presence.
* * The Ruin: Returning home, he finds his apartment completely ransacked. His art is trampled, his sculptures smashed, and his drug stash stolen.
* * The Recognition: Looking out the window, he locks eyes with his friend standing below. The friend is wearing distinct, high-status luxury shoes—the symbol of someone climbing the accelerationist ladder at his expense.
* * The Hesitation: Instead of giving chase, the protagonist freezes at his front door. Recognizing that confrontation cannot restore his destroyed work or stolen future, he surrenders to total shock and futility, realizing he has lost his synthetic armor.
* Forced Detox & Transcendent Vision
Stuck in the quiet ruin of his wrecked sanctuary, the protagonist undergoes a severe, involuntary detox. Without the chemical buffer to numb his awareness:


* The Fever & Hallucination: Physical withdrawal forces him to confront his existential grief directly while sitting among his trampled possessions.
* * The Dual Flashes: As his body shuts down, his fading consciousness rapidly cuts between the dark, gloomy decay of his apartment and the vibrant, beautiful colors of the autumn valley.
* * Shift in Perception: Striped of his ego, he no longer fears the blood-marked statues. He sees them not as foolish sacrifices, but as monuments to a time when people stood for something larger than individual survival.
* The Climax & Violent Revival
* Near-Death State: Floating between the two worlds, he feels a fleeting sense of peace, believing he is finally integrating into the timeless past and escaping the system.
* * The Technological Interception: The sirens of an approaching ambulance grow louder. The corporate state apparatus refuses to let him die, viewing his life strictly as a unit of production and debt.
* * The Awakening: Pulling him back from the edge of death, paramedics resuscitate him. He wakes up violently in the back of the ambulance, screaming in raw agony and existential horror—fully lucid, unmedicated, and trapped back inside the cold, mechanical reality he tried to escape.
