# Fleeting Utility — the story (canonical)

## The world
A near future of hyper-accelerationism. Greed has reached everywhere: there is no
untouched village, no outside, no escape hatch. Everything is leased: the apartment,
the tools, the drug that lets him work. Nothing he makes can abide, so every act has a
fleeting utility.

**The valley is nowhere.** The autumn valley of his dreams does not exist and maybe
never did. Its imagery reaches him through luxury advertising: a fashion campaign that
sells misty birch forests, fallen leaves and old blood-marked stones as heritage
"authenticity". Even his longing has been sold to him. The only real fragment is one
fenced stone in a parking lot back home.

**The colossi.** The dream statues are human colossi: 50-foot figures on stone
plinths, arms raised, a last stand frozen in stone, with blood running down their legs
and plinths. The city has its own: the corporation's **golden colossus**, a cheap copy of the
Colossus of Rhodes and the brand's mascot. It is the same figure, gaudy gold paint
over visible seams, straddling a canal and selling something. The real ones are stone and blood; the gold one is the lie.

## Prologue: Arrival
1. **The town he leaves.** It is not pastoral: it is a smaller copy of the city, further
   along in its decay. Shuttered main street, one logistics depot, fields fenced and
   leased to an agri-corp, the same billboards older and peeling, dead commerce under
   orange dust. In a parking lot stands one old stone, fenced, with a corporate logo
   bolted to it. Nobody looks at it.
2. **The city.** He rides in on a packed commuter maglev at dusk (a gliding cinematic
   camera that rides beside the train, flies through his carriage, and shakes inside it): teal-grey monolith blocks, megatowers over
   older low blocks crammed beneath, haze. The billboards are the brightest thing in the
   sky: techno-futurist promises (*Upgrade your focus. Outperform yourself.*), and among
   them the luxury campaign: a model walking a misty autumn path past blood-marked
   stones. (Players will see that image again in his dreams.) Over a canal stands the
   **golden colossus**, the brand's cheap copy of Rhodes, lit like a promise; the
   maglev runs between its legs. On his phone: *LEASE CONFIRMED. Terms apply.*
3. **The walk.** He gets off at Block 31, where the line runs along the avenue in front
   of massive blocks that hide everything behind them. He comes down the station stairs,
   a shoulder bag at his side, and crosses the whole street to the rundown side, through a
   group of ten crossing the other way to the metro; a car rides past behind him. The
   street is packed and cramped. He walks up the avenue (third person) beside the rundown
   buildings. The classes are split by a glowing median line, "DO NOT CROSS THE LINE",
   the same warning as in the carriage. Left: the clean, lit Heritage
   Collection boutique, a guard, a rope line, suits queuing. Right: tents, a trash fire,
   people slumped, a dealer huddle, two men fighting. Suits cross his path without
   looking at him; commuters pass with their heads down. At the left corner of his
   street an argument turns into a fight: a shove, a stagger, the first swing. He sees
   two seconds of it and turns into his street. At the end: his building, a decaying beige sliver wedged between taller blocks, one weak bulb over the door.
4. **The lease.** A bare, cramped room. The lease is presented and accepted. Its clauses
   are the rules of his life and teach the game diegetically:
   - *No alterations.* His works are reclaimed.
   - *Premises serviced nightly.* Sleep ends the day.
   - *Works produced on the premises remain subject to the lessor.*
   - *Renewal at the lessor's discretion.* The cycle choice.
## Night Zero: the first evening (added 2026-10-21)
The friend is introduced, liked, and quietly set up. Playable, not a cutscene: it
teaches the controls, the first dialogue choices and the free walk. Calls come
**straight into his head** (an implant, leased like everything else): voices only, no
faces, no mouths opening. Ads and the landlord can reach him the same way (beat 8).

N1. **The call.** Right after the title, at his building's door, the friend calls in
    his head. Warm, loud, old-town jokes. *"You're there? You signed? Good, good."*
    (The friend gets a **referral fee from the landlord and the corporation** for every
    lease signed. Asking whether he has signed sounds friendly. It isn't.)
N2. **The lease** (Prologue 4) is signed in the bare room.
N3. **Moving in.** The friend arrives in person and helps carry the boxes up (the
    controls tutorial: pick up, carry, place). The friend complains the whole time: the
    neighbourhood is disgusting, dangerous, *"you can't stay here long, okay? It's a
    first step."* The friend found him this unit.
N4. **The invitation.** Boxes in, the friend invites him to dinner with their corpo
    friends. They go **by car**, because corpos don't take the metro.
N5. **The other side.** An aerial shot of the rich side of the city, the mirror of the
    arrival: real style instead of brutalism, beautiful towers, shoots, brands,
    luxury cars, people on display. The line he saw on the street is the shape of the
    whole city.
N6. **The dinner.** Underdressed with his shoulder bag among suits. The corpo friends
    are vain and hollow: status, upgrades, leases, who knows whom. The first **dialogue
    choices**: he can try to fit in or be honest. Choices change tone and how they treat
    him, never the outcome (the evening ends the same way). He notices **the friend's
    shoes**: the campaign's luxury shoes, the ones he will see again through the window
    (beat 15). In his head: *was this the right decision?*
N7. **The gift.** The friend offers to drive him home; he says no, he wants to walk and
    see the city. Before he goes, the friend presses the drug on him: *"Just to take the
    edge off. Helps you adapt. Everyone's anxious the first month."* He can refuse,
    and he finds it in his bag later anyway: the friend slipped it in. The choice he was
    offered was an illusion.
N8. **The walk.** On foot through the rich side to the metro, seeing up close what the
    aerial showed from above.
N9. **The metro home.** The night train: who rides it this late, the carriage's state,
    the shift as the line crosses back into the low blocks. He walks home past his
    corner. The bag, the drug, the bare room.

## Day 1
5. **Day 1.** He is young, ambitious and alone, sure that life starts here.

## Act I: The leased life (the daily loops)
6. **Mastery in vain.** He tries to master anything he can get his hands on, painting
   first. He paints the same thing again and again: the autumn valley with a monolith,
   the campaign's image, which he believes is a memory. It is never right. On the drug
   he is sharp, fast and vivid; without it he is nothing. Every morning the lease
   reclaims the works.
7. **Status.** The city's people are fuelled by greed, jockeying, advancing. Among them
   is **the friend from the town**, the one who left first and "made it", who helped
   him find the lease. The friend dresses in the campaign's luxury.
7a. **The friend keeps calling.** Through Act I the friend checks in by head-call:
    warm, funny, always busy. Invitations, gossip from the corpo crowd, and small
    favours (*"sign this for me, it's nothing"*, *"can my guy store something at
    yours?"*). Each call feels like friendship; each quietly costs him something. By the
    ransack the player has a relationship to lose.
7b. **The neighbour.** Through the wall lives someone further along the same path:
    dependent, decayed, kind. Mostly a voice (through the wall, on the stairs, at the
    door), the mirror of his future. The neighbour notices the room getting worse before
    he admits it, and is the one person who asks how he is and means it.
7c. **Metro or park.** On free days he chooses where to go: the simulated corporate park,
    or the metro across the city. The ride is the city's barometer across the acts: more
    ads, the drug dearer (Act II), the rich side spreading, his own district sinking.
7d. **The painting sold.** The friend "borrows" one of his valley paintings and sells it
    to the corpo crowd as authentic heritage art. He gets nothing. Later the campaign's
    new image looks very like his canvas: the lie he was sold, sold again with his hand
    in it.
8. **Promises.** The billboards follow him inside as messages and offers: focus
   upgrades, subscriptions, corporate care. Can he trust them?
9. **Devolution.** When the drug wears off, overload rises and he falls into compulsive
   self-soothing in his own captivity. Each relapse erodes his belief in himself.
10. **Night.** He dreams of the valley and its colossi, 50-foot human figures in stone,
    arms raised, marked with blood: people who made a last stand for something. They
    look like the golden mascot, but they are not polished. When he reaches for the meaning, it
    slips away. He cannot imagine sacrificing himself, and he admits it: he is a selfish
    animal.
11. **Morning.** The room is a little more decayed. He tries again.

## Act II: Chemical gentrification
12. The drug is enclosed as an elite commodity and his supply shrinks. Crashes,
    compulsions and collapses into the valley come more often.

## Act III: The walk and the betrayal
13. With the room unbearable, he walks the simulated corporate park: leased air,
    licensed trees.
14. He returns to find the room ransacked: art destroyed, stash stolen, bench gone.
15. Through the window he sees the friend in high-status luxury shoes, the campaign's
    shoes. The climber sold him out.
16. **The hesitation.** He freezes at the door. Confrontation is pointless, and
    ownership was always an illusion.

16a. **The neighbour, after.** The neighbour is the one who comes to the door after
     the ransack (or who doesn't, because they have relapsed too).

## Act IV: Forced detox and dual visions
17. Stuck in the ruined room with no drug, in withdrawal. Visions cut between the
    rotting room and the valley.
18. Without the chemical ego shield, he finally sees past the advert: the stones were
    never "heritage", and the golden colossus is the lie. The stone colossi are
    **monuments** to a time when humans stood for something greater than atomic
    self-interest. For the first time, the meaning stays.

18a. **The stone.** The one real fragment of the valley, the fenced stone from the
     parking lot back home, turns up in the window of the Heritage Collection boutique,
     lit and priced. He sees it on the metro or the walk, after the realisation. Even the
     last true thing has been leased.

## Act V: Technological interception
19. He fades out, at peace.
20. A corporate ambulance (the same brand as the billboards) resuscitates him against
    his will. The promise comes due.
21. He wakes screaming, lucid and unmedicated, under clinical white light, trapped back
    inside the system.

## Epilogue: The lease renews
22. A scarred reset, then the choice:
    - **Renew the lease:** the same room, decaying faster, fewer doses, the scars
      carried forward.
    - **Walk away:** he lets the lease lapse, and someone else moves in by Monday, an
      echo of his own arrival.

## Open decisions
- His name, or deliberately none.
- The friend's name, and who they were in the town (Alain: wait). The friend's motive is
  settled: a referral fee from the landlord and the corporation.
- Faces at the dinner: stylised figures, framing (shadow, reflections, backs), or the
  rich wearing fashion-tech face veils (their faces literally a product). Proposed:
  veils.
- The brand: one conglomerate behind the billboards, the campaign, the drug and the
  ambulance? (Proposed: yes.)
- If he refuses Corporate Care (not built yet): no ambulance comes; he wakes on his own,
  alive, with no clinic, and the end card follows (decided 2026-10-26).
- What "walk away" means: leaving, giving up or dying (currently ambiguous on purpose).
- Whether the campaign's model is ever seen in person (the friend? the protagonist's
  own reflection?).
