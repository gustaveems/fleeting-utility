# Fleeting Utility — the story (canonical)

Source of truth for every narrative ticket. Built from `MASTER_BRIEF.md`, Alain's
loop text (2026-10-18) and the design decisions since. Where they disagree, this file
wins. Change it before changing the game.

## The world
A near future of hyper-accelerationism. Greed has reached everywhere: there is no
untouched village, no outside, no escape hatch. Everything is leased: the apartment,
the tools, the drug that lets him work. Nothing he makes can abide, so every act has a
fleeting utility.

**The valley is nowhere.** The autumn valley of his dreams does not exist and maybe
never did. Its imagery reaches him through luxury advertising: a fashion campaign that
sells misty birch forests, fallen leaves and old blood-marked stones as heritage
"authenticity". Even his longing has been sold to him. The only real fragment is one
fenced stone in a parking lot back home.

## Prologue: Arrival
1. **The town he leaves.** It is not pastoral: it is a smaller copy of the city, further
   along in its decay. Shuttered main street, one logistics depot, fields fenced and
   leased to an agri-corp, the same billboards older and peeling, dead commerce under
   orange dust. In a parking lot stands one old stone, fenced, with a corporate logo
   bolted to it. Nobody looks at it.
2. **The city.** A cinematic flyover at dusk: teal-grey monolith blocks, megatowers over
   older low blocks crammed beneath, haze. The billboards are the brightest thing in the
   sky: techno-futurist promises (*Upgrade your focus. Outperform yourself.*), and among
   them the luxury campaign: a model walking a misty autumn path past blood-marked
   stones. (Players will see that image again in his dreams.)
3. **The building.** The camera drops to street level (dense, filthy, neon in the haze)
   and finds his building: a decaying sliver wedged between taller blocks.
4. **The lease.** A bare, cramped room. The lease is presented and accepted. Its clauses
   are the rules of his life and teach the game diegetically:
   - *No alterations.* His works are reclaimed.
   - *Premises serviced nightly.* Sleep ends the day.
   - *Works produced on the premises remain subject to the lessor.*
   - *Renewal at the lessor's discretion.* The cycle choice.
5. **Day 1.** He is young, ambitious and alone, sure that life starts here.

## Act I: The leased life (the daily loops)
6. **Mastery in vain.** He tries to master anything he can get his hands on, painting
   first. He paints the same thing again and again: the autumn valley with a monolith,
   the campaign's image, which he believes is a memory. It is never right. On the drug
   he is sharp, fast and vivid; without it he is nothing. Every morning the lease
   reclaims the works.
7. **Status.** The city's people are fuelled by greed, jockeying, advancing. Among them
   is **the friend from the town**, the one who left first and "made it", who helped
   him find the lease. The friend dresses in the campaign's luxury.
8. **Promises.** The billboards follow him inside as messages and offers: focus
   upgrades, subscriptions, corporate care. Can he trust them?
9. **Devolution.** When the drug wears off, overload rises and he falls into compulsive
   self-soothing in his own captivity. Each relapse erodes his belief in himself.
10. **Night.** He dreams of the valley and the 50-foot statues marked with blood, of
    people who made a last stand for something. When he reaches for the meaning, it
    slips away. He cannot imagine sacrificing himself, and he admits it: he is a selfish
    animal.
11. **Morning.** The room is a little more decayed. He tries again.

## Act II: Chemical gentrification
12. The drug is enclosed as an elite commodity and his supply shrinks. Crashes,
    compulsions and collapses into the valley come more often.

## Act III: The walk and the betrayal
13. With the room unbearable, he walks the simulated corporate park: leased air,
    licensed trees.
14. He returns to find the room ransacked: art destroyed, stash stolen, bench gone.
15. Through the window he sees the friend in high-status luxury shoes, the campaign's
    shoes. The climber sold him out.
16. **The hesitation.** He freezes at the door. Confrontation is pointless, and
    ownership was always an illusion.

## Act IV: Forced detox and dual visions
17. Stuck in the ruined room with no drug, in withdrawal. Visions cut between the
    rotting room and the valley.
18. Without the chemical ego shield, he finally sees past the advert: the stones were
    never "heritage". The statues are **monuments** to a time when humans stood for
    something greater than atomic self-interest. For the first time, the meaning stays.

## Act V: Technological interception
19. He fades out, at peace.
20. A corporate ambulance (the same brand as the billboards) resuscitates him against
    his will. The promise comes due.
21. He wakes screaming, lucid and unmedicated, under clinical white light, trapped back
    inside the system.

## Epilogue: The lease renews
22. A scarred reset, then the choice:
    - **Renew the lease:** the same room, decaying faster, fewer doses, the scars
      carried forward.
    - **Walk away:** he lets the lease lapse, and someone else moves in by Monday, an
      echo of his own arrival.

## Open decisions
- His name, or deliberately none.
- The friend's name, and who he was in the town.
- The brand: one conglomerate behind the billboards, the campaign, the drug and the
  ambulance? (Proposed: yes.)
- What "walk away" means: leaving, giving up or dying (currently ambiguous on purpose).
- Whether the campaign's model is ever seen in person (the friend? the protagonist's
  own reflection?).
