# Fleeting Utility — story script (draft)

Companion to `docs/STORY.md`, which stays the canonical beat list. This file turns
the beats into a readable script: the world, the ideology the game argues with, the
characters, and the scenes in order with draft dialogue. Where this file and
`STORY.md` disagree, `STORY.md` wins. Lines marked *(draft)* are placeholders for the
writing pass and are not yet in the game.

---

## 1. Logline
A man who runs on a leased drug builds beautiful things that the lease can take back
by morning. When the drug is enclosed and his supply dies, he sees the statues of a
dream for what they are, and the system he lives in decides he is still worth keeping
alive.

## 2. Tone and rules of the writing
- **Cold surfaces, warm interior.** The city talks in brand voice: bright, upbeat,
  imperative. The protagonist talks in fragments, self-mocking and lonely.
- **Nothing is owned.** Every object, room, sentence and feeling in the game is leased,
  licensed or sold. Write nouns as contracts where possible.
- **Choices change tone, not outcome.** The dinner, the friend's gift and the
  confession all end the same way. The player's freedom is in how he carries it.
- **Ads and the lease speak in the second person.** "You" is the company's word.
  The protagonist's own thoughts use "I" or "he" only when he is close to the truth.
- **No villain speeches.** The system is never one man. It is the friend, the landlord,
  the billboard, the commuter with his head down.

## 3. Ideology

This is the part the game argues about. The regime has a worldview, and it is
coherent enough that people live by it. The protagonist believes it, then loses his
belief, then has to decide whether anything replaces it.

### 3.1 The doctrine of the system: hyper-accelerationism
The official creed, repeated on billboards, in the radio bulletin and in the friend's
mouth, can be put in four claims:

1. **Speed is the good.** Faster focus, faster output, faster turnover of everything.
   Slowness is failure. *"Upgrade your focus. Outperform yourself."*
2. **Nothing should last.** Works, rooms, relationships and people are inputs with a
   shelf life. A thing that abides is a thing that is not being used. Hence the lease
   clause *Works produced on the premises remain subject to the lessor*, and the
   ransack that is treated as routine.
3. **Ownership is a service.** You never own the tools, the room, the drug or the
   sky; you license them, and the licence renews at the lessor's discretion. Freedom
   is the ability to choose a plan, not to hold anything.
4. **The self is a product.** Your mind is the most valuable asset you lease. Focus
   upgrades, care subscriptions and the implant's call log are the same offer: keep
   yourself in working order and keep paying for it.

The doctrine has no villain, which is why it holds. It presents itself as
*progress* and as *care*. Corporate Care is the same product as the focus pill, one
week later.

### 3.2 The price: fleeting utility
Everything made under this doctrine has **fleeting utility**: it is valuable only while
it is being used and nothing outlasts the person who made it. The protagonist's art
is the clearest case. He paints the valley over and over, every morning it is
reclaimed, and nothing he makes can be kept. The city has the same problem at scale:
the golden colossus is regilded every year, the stone one is fenced and forgotten.

### 3.3 The enclosure: chemical gentrification
The drug is the regime's sacrament. It gives the focus that the doctrine demands, and
it is the first thing the system takes away from people who cannot pay. Act II makes
the ideology literal: the elite enclose the supply, the lower districts crash, and the
advert keeps selling the same focus to the people it has just locked out. The
enclosure is presented as a *market adjustment*. Nobody in the game calls it theft.

### 3.4 The lie of the valley: nature and heritage as product
The regime's answer to a longing for something older is to sell it back. The luxury
Heritage Collection campaign uses misty birches, fallen leaves and blood-marked
stones as "authenticity". Its image is taken from the protagonist's own canvas. The
valley in his dreams is therefore not a memory of a real place: it is a memory of an
advert that he has been sold, and the dream is its first owner's leftovers. The game
never says the valley is real. It says the longing is.

### 3.5 The counter-idea: the colossi
The stone colossi in his dreams are the one thing the system cannot lease. They are
human figures, 50 feet tall, arms raised, on plinths, with blood running down them: a
last stand held in stone. They are not sold, not regilded and not enclosed, and they
are the only monuments in the game that promise something outlasting the individual.

What they stand for, the game refuses to spell out. Under the drug the protagonist
cannot reach the meaning; it slips away. Off the drug, in withdrawal, he sees it
clearly for one moment: **people once stood for something greater than atomic
self-interest.** The idea is collective and costly. It asks someone to give
something up for others, and the protagonist's first honest reaction is to admit he
cannot do it. *"He cannot imagine sacrificing himself, and he admits it: he is a
selfish animal."*

### 3.6 The gold colossus and the stone one
The two statues are the ideology in one image. The gold one is the corporation's
cheap copy of the Colossus of Rhodes: the same pose as the stone colossi, gilded,
straddling a canal, selling something. It says the same thing the stone ones say, with
the blood removed and the seams painted over. The game's visual rule is the
ideology's rule: **the real colossi are stone and blood; the gold one is the lie.**

### 3.7 What the game does not resolve
- The protagonist does not become a revolutionary. Confrontation with the friend is
  pointless, and he knows it. The game ends on a lease, not a barricade.
- The colossi do not give an instruction. The meaning stays in the dream, and the
  player has to carry it out of the dream without a slogan.
- Whether the "walk away" ending is a refusal, a surrender or a death stays open on
  purpose (see `STORY.md`, open decisions).

## 4. Characters

**The protagonist.** Young, ambitious, alone, sure life starts in the city. A maker:
he paints first, then builds and assembles anything he can get hands on. Sharp and
vivid on the drug, nothing without it. Name undecided (see `STORY.md`: "His name, or
deliberately none").

**The friend.** From the same town; left first and "made it". Warm, funny, always busy,
dressed in the campaign's luxury. Helped him find the lease, and was paid a referral
fee by the landlord and the corporation for it. Calls straight into his head and asks
for small favours that each cost him something. The friend is not cruel. The friend
believes the doctrine completely and calls it friendship. Name undecided.

**The neighbour.** Through the wall, further along the same path: dependent, decayed,
kind. Mostly a voice on the stairs and at the door. The mirror of the protagonist's
future and the only one who asks how he is and means it. Notices the room getting
worse before he admits it.

**The landlord and the lessor.** Never seen. Present in the lease, the notices under
the door and the incident reports. The most complete embodiment of the ideology: a
clause, not a face.

**The corporate crowd.** Vain and hollow at the dinner: status, upgrades, leases, who
knows whom. Faces veiled in fashion-tech (proposed, undecided).

**The clinic staff.** The ambulance's crew. Calm, clinical, kind in the way that
means "you're ours now".

## 5. Scripted sequence

Scene numbers follow `STORY.md`. Dialogue is draft.

### Prologue: Arrival

**P1. The town he leaves.** Shuttered main street, one logistics depot, fields
leased to an agri-corp, billboards peeling over orange dust. In a parking lot, one
fenced stone with a corporate logo bolted to it. Nobody looks at it.
- *(Billboard, draft)* "Still here. Still leased."

**P2. The city.** Dusk, a packed commuter maglev, teal-grey monoliths, haze. The
brightest things in the sky are the billboards. A model walks a misty autumn path past
blood-marked stones. Over the canal, the golden colossus, lit like a promise. The
phone shows: *LEASE CONFIRMED. Terms apply.*
- *(Carriage sign, existing)* "DO NOT CROSS THE LINE"

**P3. The walk.** Block 31. He crosses the street, past the glowing median line.
Left: the clean Heritage Collection boutique, a guard, a rope line, suits. Right:
tents, a trash fire, people slumped, a fight at the corner. Nobody looks at him.
He turns into his street.

**P4. The lease.** A bare, cramped room. The lease is read and accepted. Its clauses
are the rules the game will teach him:
- *No alterations.* His works are reclaimed.
- *Premises serviced nightly.* Sleep ends the day.
- *Works produced on the premises remain subject to the lessor.*
- *Renewal at the lessor's discretion.*

### Night Zero: the first evening

**N1. The call.** At the building door, a voice in his head.
> FRIEND: You're there? You signed? Good, good.

**N2. Signing.** The lease, again, in the bare room.

**N3. Moving in.** The friend arrives in person and carries boxes with him.
> FRIEND: You can't stay here long, okay? It's a first step.

**N4. The invitation.** The friend invites him to dinner with the corporate crowd.
They go by car, because corpos don't take the metro.

**N5. The other side.** Aerial shot of the rich side: real style, beautiful towers,
brands, people on display. The line he saw on the street is the shape of the whole
city.

**N6. The dinner.** Underdressed, shoulder bag among suits. The corporate crowd
talks about status. The first dialogue choice: fit in, or be honest. The friend's
shoes are the campaign's luxury shoes. In his head: *was this the right decision?*
- *(Crowd, draft)* "Upgrade or be upgraded."

**N7. The gift.** The friend offers a ride home. He says he will walk. The friend
presses the drug on him.
> FRIEND: Just to take the edge off. Helps you adapt. Everyone's anxious the first month.

He can refuse. He finds it in his bag later anyway. *The choice he was offered was an
illusion.*

**N8–N9. The walk and the metro home.** Through the rich side on foot, then the night
train back to the low blocks. The bag, the drug, the bare room.

### Day 1 and Act I: the leased life

**6. Mastery in vain.** Daily loops. He paints the valley with its monolith; it is never
right. The lease reclaims the work every morning. The campaign's image is the thing he
believes is a memory.
- *(Self, draft)* "Again. Nearly. Again."

**7. Status.** The crowd's rooftop. The friend in the luxury. A standing that sinks
every morning, and invitations that stop once the park walk is taken.

**7a. The friend keeps calling.** Invitations, gossip, small favours:
> FRIEND: Sign this for me. It's nothing.
> FRIEND: Can my guy store something at yours?

Each call feels like friendship. Each quietly costs him something.

**7b. The neighbour.** A voice through the wall, on the stairs.
> NEIGHBOUR: The room's getting to you, isn't it. It's getting to all of us.

**7c. Metro or park.** On free days, the door is a menu: the metro across the city or
the simulated corporate park. The ride is the city's barometer. More ads, dearer
drug, the rich side spreading, his own district sinking.

**7d. The painting sold.** The friend borrows a valley canvas and sells it to the
crowd as authentic heritage art. He gets nothing. Later the campaign's new image looks
like his canvas.
> FRIEND: It's a favour. You'd have sold it for nothing anyway.

**8. Promises.** Billboards become messages inside the room.
- *(Focus+, draft)* "Focus+ Pro. Three days free. Keep it, or cancel it. Your choice."

**8a. Paper under the door.** The lessor's reminders, inspections and small print.
The Heritage Collection flyer with the campaign's shoes on it.
- *(Notice, draft)* "Works remain subject to the lessor. Thank you for your cooperation."

**8b. The radio.** One bulletin a day: the city's news, which is the brand's news. The
golden colossus is regilded. The drug is enclosed. The Heritage Quarter spreads.
- *(Bulletin, draft)* "Good news for the city. Upgrade your city."

**8c. LeaseLink.** His implant keeps a log of every call. In the valley it has no
signal.

**9. Devolution.** The drug wears off. Overload rises. Compulsive self-soothing, in his
own captivity. Each relapse erodes his self-belief.
- *(Self, draft, after a relapse)* "It's fine. It's fine. It's fine."

**10. Night.** The valley, the colossi, the blood on their legs. The meaning starts
to form and then slips.

**11. Morning.** The room is a little more decayed. He tries again.

### Act II: chemical gentrification

**12.** The drug is enclosed as an elite commodity. The supply shrinks. Crashes,
compulsions and collapses into the valley come more often. The bulletin calls it
"a better class of focus".

### Act III: the walk and the betrayal

**13.** The park. Leased air, licensed trees, a bench that is not his.

**14.** He comes home. The room is ransacked. The art is destroyed, the stash is gone,
the bench is gone.
- *(Incident report, draft)* "The contents removed were the lessor's property."

**15.** Through the window he sees the friend in the campaign's shoes. The climber sold
him out.

**16. The hesitation.** He freezes at the door. Confrontation is pointless, and
ownership was always an illusion.

**16a. The neighbour, after.** Either at the door, or not, because the neighbour has
relapsed too.

### Act IV: forced detox and dual visions

**17.** Withdrawal in the ruined room. Visions cut between the rotting room and the
valley.

**18. The stone.** Without the chemical ego shield, he sees past the advert: the stones
were never heritage, and the golden colossus is the lie. The stone colossi are
monuments to a time when humans stood for something greater than atomic self-interest.
The meaning stays this time.

**18a. The fenced stone.** The one real fragment of the valley appears in the window of
the Heritage Collection boutique, lit and priced.
- *(Price tag, draft)* "Heritage Stone, authentic. Limited edition."

### Act V: technological interception

**19.** He fades out, at peace.

**20.** A corporate ambulance, the same brand as the billboards, resuscitates him
against his will.
> CREW: Stay with us. You're covered. You're ours now.

**21.** He wakes screaming, lucid and unmedicated, under clinical white light.
The promise comes due.

### Epilogue: the lease renews

**22.** A scarred reset, then the choice:
- **Renew the lease.** The same room, decaying faster, fewer doses, the scars carried
  forward. The lease reads: *Renewal at the lessor's discretion.*
- **Walk away.** He lets the lease lapse. By Monday someone else moves in, an echo of
  his own arrival.

*(Alternate, decided 2026-10-26, built in Ticket-23: if he refused Corporate Care, no
ambulance comes. He wakes on his own, alive, with no clinic. The card reads "No one
came. He woke anyway.")*

## 6. The protagonist's paintings

The paintings he makes on the Easel are his own work, and they are the clearest
evidence of his skill. Two reference canvases from the project's mood board set
their range:

- **The tiger in the river** (`docs/references/painting_tiger_river.jpg`): saturated,
  loose and alive. This is his work on the drug: fast, vivid and confident. The
  brushwork is the "high-focus" look in paint.
- **The wolves among the flowers** (`docs/references/painting_wolves_flowers.jpg`):
  warmer and earthier, with bones at the bottom of the frame. This is the work he
  makes when he is sliding: animal, decaying and quiet, with the bones as a small
  memento mori.

Neither is the valley. The valley is what he keeps trying to paint and cannot get
right; these are what he can paint, and the lease takes them the same way.

*(Draft, for the writing pass: the friend borrows the valley canvas, not these. The
ransack destroys these two. Both are placeholders for the Easel's finished works,
not shipped game art.)*

## 7. Open for the writing pass
- Names: the protagonist, the friend, the neighbour (see `STORY.md`).
- Faces at the dinner: veils, shadow, reflections or backs.
- Whether the campaign's model is ever seen in person.
- Whether the colossi ever speak. Proposed: never. They only show.
- The ideology's own voice: does the regime get one slogan that repeats, or several
  that contradict? Proposed: one, the billboard line, so the player learns it by
  repetition.
